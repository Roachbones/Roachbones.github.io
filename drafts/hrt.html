Sort the effects into pros and cons. If the pros outweigh the cons, start HRT.

That's it. If you want the effects of swapping your hormones, swap your hormones. If you don't, don't. In general, if you would like the effects of an action, do that action. This is painfully obvious, but many people, including myself, need to hear it at times.

The rest of this document addresses two common misconceptions that prevent people from acting on their preferences: the misconception that gender is real, and the misconception that HRT is a big deal.

Gender is fake

Please do not think “I should only take estrogen if I know I'm really a woman inside” or anything similarly nonsensical. Disregard the labels “man” and “woman”. They don't mean anything. You might as well be making decisions based on astrology or MBTI or your Homestuck classpect.

Some find value in the man's-brain-in-a-woman's-body narrative. I completely disregard it. Why should I care about the hidden truth of my Brain Gender, even if I believed in such a thing? If I want to take estrogen, I'll take estrogen. It's not as if the mental phantasm of Gender pharmaceutically interacts with hormones. I don't need to be Really A Woman On The Inside to want soft skin instead of a receding hairline. Just do what you want.

Customizing your hormones is really not a big deal

Some women who suffer acne are prescribed spironolactone, a testosterone blocker common in the US, since testosterone can be a cause of (oilier skin, and therefore) acne. In other words, these people are irritated by an effect of testosterone, so they take a pill to get rid of the testosterone, so the effect goes away.

This is considered totally normal and not a big deal. Yet when someone with an M on their birth certificate does it, it's suddenly weird to some. Likewise it's normal for a cis man to take testosterone, but it's suddenly weird for anyone else to, because they are “supposed to” have low testosterone? There is no such thing as supposing to. If you want effects of testosterone, take testosterone. If want to stop effects of testosterone, replace it with estrogen. You're not defying some grand, divine, Correct plan by doing that. Just do what you want.

I think sex ed should teach teens about HRT, so the horrified ones can opt out of default puberty. I think many more people would, if they knew they could. Instead, most people never realize it's an option.


This blogpost has a lot of redundancy and obviousness and won't apply to most people, but I've known many people who needed to realize that they could just take estrogen, who ended up regretting not doing it sooner. If this post helps even one person do what they want, it was worth writing.
